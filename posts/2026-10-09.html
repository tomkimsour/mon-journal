Today's robot gains come from fixing what surrounds the policy (the state handed between skills, the checks before an action runs, and how human motion and intent are translated) rather than from bigger end-to-end models.

Across manipulation, navigation and humanoids, the papers with the clearest results leave the core policy mostly alone and work on its interfaces. Long-horizon chains break at skill seams because of the scene state one skill leaves for the next. Frozen VLAs are being wrapped with monitors, gates and residuals instead of being retrained. Navigation and manipulation harnesses keep large models for decisions and hand motion to dedicated tools. World-action models improve when they predict compact, control-relevant futures instead of pixels. Human demonstrations transfer when they are mapped into a shared latent or contact-event space. On the HRI side, pointing, handling, short intent-style instructions and negations all need explicit grounding, and cues that predict approach on one robot do not reliably carry over to another site. For a team building robots for people of different ages and cultures, the practical message is to instrument and test the interfaces (between skills, between model and actuator, between person and robot) at least as carefully as the policy itself.

Insights

  1. Measure the handoff, not just the skill. ManiUnit reports that joint-configuration perturbations at a skill's start cut success by about 56%, the OSS study traces seam failures mainly to displaced scene state, and SkillWeave reaches 87% composition efficiency only after steering handoffs toward states the next skill saw in training. Try recording the full scene state at every skill boundary in your long-horizon runs and replaying each skill from those recorded starts. If one skill accounts for most chain failures, fix the predecessor's end state before retraining the successor.
  2. A cheap failure trigger may already be sitting in your policy. RESETTLE fires recovery when two independently sampled action proposals keep disagreeing, and reports 74.04-93.57% lower monitoring latency than a VLM-based monitor. Diffusion and flow VLAs already sample, so logging the disagreement between two samples costs one extra forward pass and gives you a signal to correlate with failures before building anything more complex. PIER's results are a useful counterweight: a threshold gate cannot catch stale or falsely reassuring inputs, so pair any gate with a freshness check on the observation.
  3. Do not assume that social cues validated on one robot or site carry over. The FRPR study finds that pose cues improved approach anticipation in one transfer direction and not the other, that head pitch reversed its meaning between datasets, and that models detected at most 17% of target interactions with source-chosen thresholds. IAE shows pointing is still the hardest case at 16.9% strict success. For a team deploying with different age groups and cultures, budget for a small labeled validation set per deployment population and report transfer in both directions. Separately, FearCaut shows that steering a VLM's internal 'fear' direction raised hazard recall from 27.0% to 75.7% by shifting the decision threshold, not by improving discrimination. Any VLM judging people or risk should be audited for where its threshold sits, not only for accuracy.
  4. Treat wrist-camera mounting as a deployment variable. WARP-VLA reports that wrist-view perturbations drop pi-0.5 to 39.2% on LIBERO (78.3% with its adaptation), VersaCamVLA targets changes in camera count and pose, and task scrubbing finds that VLM backbones differ widely in how much they latch onto viewpoint and background shortcuts. Before trusting a VLA on a second robot of the same model, remount the wrist camera slightly and rerun a small evaluation set.

Diagnosing and Recovering from Observation-Space Shift at Long-Horizon Skill Seams RO
Pranav Wagh, Yu Fang, Yue Yang, Mingyu Ding · arXiv:2610.10810
Why read it
It turns 'long-horizon chains are brittle' into a specific, testable diagnosis that applies to any team chaining VLA or diffusion skills.
Key idea
Use privileged simulator resets to find which part of the inherited state breaks the downstream skill; the answer is displaced scene state, so detect the stall, restore the scene, then resume.
Evidence
On BOSS-44, full-chain success rises from 7.6% to 26.5%, 51% of a privileged restoration oracle, while best-of-K resampling, Diffusion Policy and world-model baselines fail to recover the evaluated seams. A real Franka with a fine-tuned pi-0.5 recovers some otherwise-terminal failures.
Caveat
The authors present the system as evidence for the diagnosis, not a general method; on the real robot the progress monitor is limited by exterior-camera observability, and real-world gains are not quantified in the abstract.
Instance-anchored interaction evidence: Grounding robot plans in human pointing and handling RO
Xinliang Xiao, Bowen Yang, Wenjing Zhang, Li Yang et al. · arXiv:2610.12157
Why read it
Directly about assistive HRI: acting on what a person pointed at or handled, which large VLMs still get badly wrong.
Key idea
Register every object in the final scene, track its identity backward through the video, and describe each frame by hand and forearm geometry relative to those instances; then decode plans with a grammar-constrained program.
Evidence
On 1,255 WatchAct requests, 64.2% plan success on implicit-intent tasks versus 27.5% for a 32B VLM, and 46.4% versus 27.0% on restoration, reversal and imitation. Given IAE's evidence as text, the same language model reaches 57.4%, so most of the gain comes from the evidence representation.
Caveat
Evaluated by symbolic execution on a benchmark, not on a physical robot; pointing remains weak at 16.9% strict success.
Fixed-Reference Pose Residuals for Measuring Cross-Dataset Cue Transfer in Human-Robot Interaction Anticipation RO
Bowen Yang, Xinliang Xiao, Wenjing Zhang, Li Yang et al. · arXiv:2610.12245
Why read it
One of the few papers today that measures whether HRI cues transfer between robots and sites, which matters directly for deploying across populations.
Key idea
Freeze a geometry-only predictor and learn pose as an additive logit correction, so each prediction splits exactly into a geometry term and a pose term and transfer of each cue can be measured.
Evidence
Between HUI360 and SSUP-A, pose raised AP from 0.277 to 0.321 in one direction and gave no measurable gain in the other; whether a person faces the camera kept its meaning across datasets while head pitch reversed; neural models detected at most 17% of target interactions with source-chosen thresholds.
Caveat
Only two egocentric datasets; the authors note simple geometric baselines and tree ensembles were competitive or better, so this is a measurement tool, not a deployable predictor.
VioLA: Learning Generalist Humanoid Control Policies from Human Data RO
Mert Albaba, Jens Beißwenger, Anna Manasyan, Daniel Marta et al. · arXiv:2610.12435
Why read it
Shows that a humanoid generalist trained mostly on human data can follow locomotion instructions on real hardware without task-specific fine-tuning.
Key idea
Have the policy predict body and hand motion latents executed by pretrained controllers; shared encoders map human and robot motion into the same latents, so human recordings become labeled action data.
Evidence
A pool of 140.6 million frames, 93.2% human; 100% zero-shot locomotion success on the real robot versus 16.7% for GR00T N1.7 and 0% for Psi_0; 88.6% manipulation success without task-specific fine-tuning; works across two VLA and one WAM backbone.
Caveat
Depends on pretrained body and hand controllers whose quality bounds the result; the abstract does not name the humanoid or give the number of locomotion or manipulation trials.
CSF: Contextual Safety Filtering for Motion Generators RO
Lizhi Yang, Yiling Hou, Yao Tang, Junheng Li et al. · arXiv:2610.12467
Why read it
A training-free safety layer for whole-body motion generators that accounts for whether a motion targets an object or a person, demonstrated on a real G1 around people.
Key idea
Ground natural-language safety rules in safe and unsafe reference trajectories from the generator itself, turn them into an affine safety value, and enforce it with a CBF-QP.
Evidence
Across four pretrained generators, the intended rules fire in all explicit and scene-triggered unsafe cases, danger-event rate drops by up to 90%, and 88-100% of benign motions are preserved; demonstrated on a real Unitree G1 in human and object interaction scenarios.
Caveat
Rules must be written in advance, and the abstract gives no quantitative real-robot results; with up to 12% of benign motions blocked, the filter may be too conservative in some interactive settings.

  • OmniHOI: Dexterous Hand-Object Interaction from Monocular Human Video RO
    Ting Mao, Yanming Shao, Ziheng Wang, Haoyu Liu et al. · arXiv:2610.10855
    Abstract

    Monocular videos of human manipulation provide abundant dexterous demonstrations, yet reconstructing hand-object interaction from a single view and transferring it to robot hands remain difficult, limiting their direct use for robot execution. Prior methods either require task-specific RL training, limiting scalability, or assume clean motion-capture trajectories and thus cannot operate directly on video. We present OmniHOI, a pipeline that turns an RGB video of hand-object interaction into an interaction-faithful trajectory on dexterous hands. The key idea is to enforce physical consistency using the evidence available at each stage: image evidence during reconstruction, contact geometry during retargeting, and dynamics during physics-in-the-loop refinement. Each stage optimizes the corresponding representation directly, correcting errors before they propagate downstream or must be absorbed by a learned policy. Across 150 motion-capture trajectories transferred to each of five dexterous hands with 6 to 22 DoF, we achieve 39-89% success, compared with at most 31% for prior transfer methods. On 60 monocular video clips, we achieve 53% success, compared with 28% for the best prior video-to-robot pipeline. Its trajectories also execute on a real bimanual robot across diverse tasks.

  • FOCUS: From Privileged States to RGB-D with Controlled Modality Switching and Representation Alignment RO
    Filip Grigorov, Kourosh Darvish, Nandita Vijaykumar · arXiv:2610.11119
    Abstract

    Vision-based reinforcement learning for robotic manipulation is sample-inefficient because RGB-D observations are high-dimensional and noisy. Privileged state information available in simulation can accelerate training, but its absence at test time creates a train-test modality gap. We propose FOCUS, a single-stage PPO framework that trains the critic on privileged state while automatically regulating whether the actor collects rollouts from RGB-D or privileged-state latents. Regulation is driven by the KL divergence between the action distributions induced by the two modalities, while representation alignment encourages consistent action selection across them. Together, these mechanisms limit RGB-D rollouts when the actor's action distributions from RGB-D and privileged-state latents disagree. As they align, RGB-D exposure increases, shifting on-policy training toward the RGB-D inputs used at test time. Across five manipulation tasks, FOCUS raises average test success from 0.71 to 0.93 relative to the strongest RGB-D-at-test baseline on each task. When accounting for each method's complete training pipeline, budget-normalized training-success AUC increases from 0.47 to 0.65. On Pick-and-Place, test success rises from 0.47 to 0.86, while AUC increases from 0.12 to 0.61, a 5.0x improvement in learning efficiency over the fixed interaction budget.

  • How Firm Should a Grasp Be? RO/CV

    Abstract

    An ideal robot grasp is firm enough to securely handle an object, yet gentle enough to avoid damaging it.
