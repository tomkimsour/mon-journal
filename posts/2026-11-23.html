Today's papers find manipulation policies that succeed without doing what was asked or when it was asked: they ignore the instruction, act on state that went stale while people moved, and only benchmarks that score the instruction, the timing and the contact catch it.

Many of today's papers check whether a robot's success means what it seems to mean, and often it does not. ManipScope finds that ten policy checkpoints follow the reassigned instruction on 3.9% of states, worse than a chooser that ignores language. Two training papers and a VLM safety audit trace the same shortcut to visual priors that crowd out the instruction. New benchmarks add what clean, synchronous success rates leave out: a world that keeps moving during inference, contact with distractors, and the physical act of pressing keys. The human-facing work treats time as the safety variable. A robot agent writes its own interrupt for when a person steps in, a navigation planner puts a deadline on a plan while the LLM is still thinking, and a swarm safety filter breaks once neighbour estimates are older than about 150 ms. For a team building robots that live among people of different ages and cultures, the advice is to test whether the robot followed this person's request, at this moment, and score the contacts it made along the way.

Insights

  1. Test whether the robot follows the person, not the scene. ManipScope's target-reassignment protocol is simple to copy: keep the initial state, change which object is named, and count how often the policy changes its choice. That ten checkpoints fall below an instruction-blind chooser, while a GPT-6 agent reaches 88% on the same task, suggests a split design: let an agent select the target and the policy execute, which the authors report partly restores instruction following. For robots that take requests from children, older adults or non-native speakers, run this test with your users' own phrasings.
  2. Treat staleness as a first-class safety quantity. The swarm study's finding that a constant wider safety margin makes collisions more frequent in a bottleneck, while a constant-velocity predictor at the CBF extends the tolerated frame period by 1.4-1.7 times, argues for compensating delay where safety is enforced rather than padding margins. The staleness-deadline paper adds a sobering number for people: the robot never collides while moving, but pedestrians who never yield walk into the stopped robot in 23% of offline and 32% of live missions. Stopping is not a complete safety policy around people.
  3. For HRI, model the interaction pair, not only the user. The autism study finds that children with ASD and their caregivers share three acoustic markers of engagement during home socially assistive robot sessions, with children varying more between states. EIP models speech and motion jointly so turn-taking and gesture stay coupled, and Ego2Ego generates what a collaborator sees from their trajectory. A robot working with a child and a parent can read engagement from both voices. It should not rely on the child's signal alone, which is also the noisier one.
  4. Deterministic ROS 2 execution is now available without changing application code. The out-of-order parallel simulator runs existing ROS 2 applications deterministically, up to 2.6 times faster than synchronous parallel simulation and with an order of magnitude less message memory than asynchronous simulation. If your CI has flaky integration tests that pass on one machine and fail on another, this is worth trying before adding more retries.

Tripwire: Robot Agents that Program Their Own Interrupts under Human Disturbance RO/CV
Sunli Chen, Yun Zong, Qinhong Zhou, Xiaowen Qiu et al. · arXiv:2611.27182
Why read it
It addresses the exact gap between a slow agent and a person who steps into the robot's workspace, and it needs no training, so it can be added to an existing VLA stack.
Key idea
With each policy call, the agent writes a small stateful Python program (a tripwire) that runs alongside the policy, can query a compact VLM, for example to measure a person's distance, and returns control to the agent when its condition fires.
Evidence
On RoboHD, 168 RoboCasa365 scenes with a simulated person disturbing the robot and any human contact counted as failure, tested with three VLAs and one WAM: against a turn-based harness with the same agent and tools, it halves the human-contact rate with the strongest policy, raising safety from 61.3% to 81.3%, with task success reported as 38.7% versus 31.0%. Real-robot runs complete two manipulation tasks under human interference where the turn-based harness and the policy alone largely fail.
Caveat
The quantitative results come from a simulated person, and the real-robot evidence is two tasks without reported rates. The tripwire is only as reliable as the compact VLM's person detection, whose latency and miss rate the abstract does not give.
ManipScope: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Instruction Following and Generalization in Robotic Manipulation RO
Jiacheng Li, Yaping Li, Congsheng Xu, Qingchen Liu et al. · arXiv:2611.27305
Why read it
It gives a cheap protocol that shows whether a policy listens, and today's numbers suggest many do not.
Key idea
Reuse trained skills under changed requests and run a chain of diagnostics, each testing one explanation for a failure, including reassigning the target on identical initial states.
Evidence
48 held-out scenes across six task families built on RoboTwin 2.0. For twelve checkpoints, 84.6% of failures in 13,200 episodes displace a distractor and gripper records show grasps of non-targets. Ten checkpoints follow every instruction on 3.9% of 1,000 states against 16% for an instruction-blind chooser and 88% for a GPT-6 agent. The failure also appears on unmodified training tasks, and two checkpoints trained on the same data do follow color instructions.
Caveat
Simulation only, built on one asset library, and policies are trained only on unmodified RoboTwin 2.0 demonstrations. Policies trained on more varied language may behave differently, and the abstract does not name the checkpoints.
Acoustic Markers of Engagement in Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder and Their Caregivers RO
Young Suh Hong, Kaleen Shrestha, Austin Joel Dympep, Maja Matarić · arXiv:2611.26810
Why read it
It is the day's most direct HRI and psychology paper: real home sessions with a socially assistive robot, children with autism, and caregivers, analysed with mixed-effects statistics.
Key idea
Model engagement from both the child's and the caregiver's vocalizations, since caregivers facilitate the interaction at home, and test which acoustic features separate engagement from disengagement in each group.
Evidence
Linear mixed-effects models on home-based SAR intervention data find three shared markers of engagement: mean Alpha Ratio, mean Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio and mean spectral slope 500-1500 Hz, consistent with cleaner, smoother voice during engagement. A State x Group interaction model shows children have significantly larger state-related differences than caregivers on all three.
Caveat
The abstract does not give the number of families, sessions or the language spoken, and these are associations within one intervention dataset, not a validated real-time engagement detector.
Embodied Interaction Policy: A Unified Model for Full-Duplex Human-Robot Interaction RO
Youqiang Gui, Yunfei Liu, Lijian Lin, Ye Zhu et al. · arXiv:2611.27327
Why read it
Social robots that talk while they move need speech and gesture to stay coordinated when a person interrupts, and this paper builds them as one streaming model.
Key idea
Couple an audio-interaction expert and a motion expert through layer-wise joint attention, with a chunk-wise causal mask and a vocal token carrying speaking state and the emotion of the response.
Evidence
Trained on EI-Data, built from co-speech and dyadic interaction data, in two stages. Motion quality improves over gesture-generation baselines. Against a cascaded system, it reports higher interaction success and 64% lower warm-start latency. Physical deployment and user studies report improved motion naturalness and interaction comfort.
Caveat
The abstract gives no participant numbers, robot platform or effect sizes for the user studies, and it does not say whether gestures or emotion conditioning were tested across cultures where gesture norms differ.
One Behavior, Out of Order: Deterministic Execution and Parallel Simulation of ROS 2 Applications RO
Chengzi Huang, Behnam Khodabandeloo, Duc Anh Nguyen, Chuanchao Gao et al. · arXiv:2611.26676
Why read it
Non-reproducible ROS 2 test runs cost every ROS 2 team time, and this works on existing applications without modifying them or ROS 2.
Key idea
Have each component, event-driven ones included, read inputs and write outputs at predefined time instants, which makes behaviour deterministic. Use the known dependencies between activations to run them out of order in parallel.
Evidence
On industrial use cases it is up to 2.6 times faster than synchronous parallel simulation and needs an order of magnitude less memory for messages than asynchronous parallel simulation. It is also evaluated on synthetic graphs.
Caveat
Determinism comes from changing when components read and write, so behaviour in simulation may differ from the free-running asynchronous system on the robot. The abstract does not name the industrial use cases or the supported ROS 2 distributions.

  • MomentMem: Memorizing Key Moments for Robot Manipulation RO/AI
    Guangyu Zhao, Yongchang Zhang, Ruizhang Jiang, Zhihong Yu et al. · arXiv:2611.26745
    Abstract

    Robotic manipulation often requires remembering visual information that is no longer observable, such as the original location of an object. Existing approaches typically rely on full observation histories, which become redundant over long horizons, or text summaries, which may omit precise visual details. We study visual moment memory, the ability to retain such details from key moments for subsequent manipulation. To support this study, we introduce VMBench, a suite of long-horizon manipulation tasks requiring memory of spatial locations, visual appearances, and their associations. We further propose MomentMem, a hierarchical architecture in which a VLM-based memorizer selects keyframes and an executor predicts actions conditioned on the selected memories. This separation enables us to evaluate memory selection alongside closed-loop task success and to study memorizer generalization independently of the executor. On VMBench, MomentMem achieves 49.9% average success, outperforming the strongest evaluated baseline by 31.7 percentage points. Moreover, we find that the memorizer transfers well to unseen appearances zero-shot. It can also adapt to an unseen task with only a few memory annotations, reaching 43.7% success, close to the 44.3% obtained with full memory supervision. Further experiments show competitive performance on RMBench and improvements in real-robot manipulation. These results highlight the value of retaining sparse visual evidence for memory-dependent manipulation. Our code is available at this https URL.

  • Mechanistic Analysis of Unified Proximity-Tactile Observation by a Capacitive Fingertip Array Constrained by a Metal Phalanx RO
    Shun Cheng · arXiv:2611.26950
    Abstract

    A capacitive fingertip array on a metal phalanx under thin silicone skin senses centimetre-scale conductive targets by fringing fields and contact force by dielectric compression. Electrode-to-body parasitics exceed the useful proximity increment by one to three orders of magnitude, and crosstalk and time-division-multiplexing (TDM) settling prevent identifying the capacitance-to-digital converter (CDC) reading with the electrode-target capacitance. The array is modelled as a multi-conductor system (sensing electrodes, driven-shield nodes, phalanx ground and an external target). The CDC measurand follows from the Maxwell capacitance matrix, with each inactive termination in the observation operator. Three mechanisms are examined: driven-shield hiding of shield-branch current and gain/phase-mismatch leakage; distinct far- and near-field operators; and TDM settling as a frame-rate limit set by shield load. A single layer used for both proximity and force reads Delta C = Delta C_force + Delta C_dielectric(epsilon_target), so its force map cannot be material-independent. A dual-array stack is analysed: the outer array is grounded as a Faraday screen during contact, and the inner array reads silicone-gap compression. FEM simulations give a monotonic, nonlinear contact response (4.6 fF at 0.05 mm to 370 fF at 0.5 mm on a 1.08 pF base), a nylon-versus-metal difference of at most 0.3 fF at exported-matrix resolution (not mesh-converged), mutual-term cancellation under driven-shield readout, and about 1 fF crosstalk on the coarse mesh. In proximity mode, electrode-shield coupling of 1.47 pF makes 10^{-3}-10^{-4} mismatch a quantitative gate: a 15.9 fF increment at 5 mm equals the leakage at |epsilon_A| ~ 10^{-2}. Force map, mechanical crosstalk and all bench quantities remain unvalidated; the equations and FEM evidence are a baseline for prototype calibration, not measured performance claims.

    Mengting Yu, Shuiying Xiang, Changjian Xie, Yonghang Chen et al. · arXiv:2611.27129
    Abstract

  • HermiteTwin: Bicubic Hermite Shell Digital Twins for Contact-Rich Deformable Manipulation RO
    Kangrui Zhang, Mu Huang, Xing Shen, Tiantian Liu et al. · arXiv:2611.27282
    Abstract

    While existing methods for reconstructing deformable object manipulation have made impressive progress, they typically represent a thin object with a single particle set that must serve two purposes at once: modeling bending, and resolving contact with the robot. To bridge this gap, we present HermiteTwin, a framework that reconstructs a physical digital twin of a thin deformable object from multiview video of a robot manipulating it. Our key idea is to represent deformation and contact separately, each at its own resolution. The object is simulated as a bicubic Hermite shell, a coarse grid of $C^1$ elements whose nodal positions and derivatives define a smooth surface with Kirchhoff--Love bending, while the gripper acts through dense samples in material space that are captured, clamped, and released, and whose attachment forces reach the shell through the Hermite basis. This decoupling keeps the physical state compact while contact stays fine-grained. We identify effective material and contact parameters with CMA-ES followed by adjoint-based refinement, and drive a reconstructed Gaussian appearance model with the simulated shell. In experiments on real cloth and foam manipulation, HermiteTwin outperforms the state-of-the-art spring--mass twin across reconstruction, resimulation, and future prediction.

  • Latent Jacobian Steer: Eliciting Contact-Rich Out-of-Distribution Behaviors from Pretrained Humanoid Controllers RO
    S K Swaminathan, Shivam Sood, Shreyansh Kansal, Chengyang He et al. · arXiv:2611.27338
    Abstract

    Given a pretrained general latent-conditioned whole-body humanoid controller, say a behavioral foundation model (BFM), how can we elicit complex and contact-rich out-of-distribution behaviors? Raw joint-action exploration enables direct, task-specific adjustments but can disrupt coordination learned by the pretrained controller, whereas latent exploration retains that coordination but is difficult to steer toward desired physical effects. To combine the complementary strengths of these exploration spaces, we propose Latent Jacobian Steer (LJS), which couples sampling in direct joint-action space with the semantics of the learned frozen controller's input latent space. For a task specified through desired physical effects, we sample task-relevant joint-action perturbations and map them through a damped local inverse of the policy Jacobian to obtain candidate latent ($z$) commands. These commands are executed through the frozen pretrained policy, while exploration is directed toward the physical outcomes required by the new task. Candidate outcomes are evaluated in simulation and composed into latent-command sequences that accomplish the task. Ablations show that neither action-space nor latent-space exploration alone provides the same capability as their coupling. Across diverse out-of-distribution tasks, simulation-based searching using this method produces contact-rich behaviors, including hurdling, manipulating articulated objects, and opening doors through non-prehensile contact. Over ten seeds, LJS completes more of each humanoid task than when searching in either space alone. Beyond humanoids, it also steers a goal-conditioned dexterous hand policy through four assembly tasks better than searching in goal space directly.

  • NormFlowSDE: Normalizing Flows for Amortized Bayesian State Estimation RO
    Yi Zhang, Lars Mikelsons · arXiv:2611.27362
    Abstract

    Bayesian state estimators, such as Kalman filters, estimate states from noisy observations based on dynamical models. However, obtaining accurate models can be challenging when the system involves effects that are difficult to model faithfully, such as friction, impacts, and deformation in contact-rich manipulation. Uncertainty in system parameters, inputs, and disturbances, together with simplified noise models, can further introduce simulation-to-reality mismatch and degrade estimation accuracy. These limitations motivate learning a distribution over state trajectories conditioned on observations and controls without explicitly specifying the underlying physical models. We introduce NormFlowSDE, an amortized Bayesian state estimator that learns a distribution over state trajectories by combining neural stochastic differential equations with conditional normalizing flows. Its key design choice is to utilize the capability of normalizing flows to learn stochastic behavior of variables, while leveraging the continuous-time dynamics of SDEs to model the evolution of states over time. Once trained, the model draws state paths for new measurement sequences without per-sequence optimization. In addition, we connect NormFlowSDE to stochastic continuous-time dynamics by proving weak convergence of a mean-centered, drift-corrected inference scheme to an Itô diffusion under stated assumptions. Finally, we benchmark NormFlowSDE against direct estimators and learned particle filters based on normalizing flows on partially observed dynamical systems, including simulated systems and real-world robot datasets. The results show that NormFlowSDE attains the lowest estimation error with calibrated uncertainty intervals on both robot datasets and filters four times faster than the fastest particle filter on every dataset.
