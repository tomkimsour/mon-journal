2026-11-02 → 2026-11-08 · 5 daily briefings · 815 of 2437 new papers matched a bucket

The week's recurring lesson was that scores can mislead: offline metrics, in-distribution verifiers and nominal benchmarks all rank policies differently from the robot.

Across 2 to 6 November, the papers kept returning to the gap between what a team measures and what the robot or the person experiences. Checkpoint selection by validation loss or pass@1 was shown to rank policies backwards on hardware (2 and 3 November), and verifier-tied benchmark gains were shown to vanish when criteria shifted slightly (6 November). In parallel, latency, tactile and reflex work moved signals into the control loop, and human-facing studies showed that perceived safety, workload and care can move independently of physical or task metrics. Human data was judged by embodiment match rather than volume, and runtime monitors and safety filters began to carry explicit bounds or guarantees. For a ROS 2 team, the consistent response is to test on the robot in closed loop, hold out a verifier or stimulus variant, and record the human-side outcome beside task success.

Trends

Offline and in-distribution scores keep misranking policies, so select checkpoints on closed-loop evidence

The signal showed up on 2 November, when offline metrics tracked real-robot success in only five of 67 cases (2610.40828), and again on 3 November, when the lowest hold-out error policy won 3 of 110 rollouts (2611.00283). On 5 November a rollout-free checkpoint ranking (2611.05838) was offered as a cheaper filter, and on 6 November verifier-tied gains disappeared under small criteria changes (2611.08318). The common advice is to rank on repeated closed-loop rollouts and to hold out a verifier variant.

Latency, tactile and reflex signals are being moved into the control loop rather than added as extra inputs

On 3 November, path-preserving retiming (2611.01390), cached semantic anchors (2611.01011) and a tactile forecasting loss (2611.02677) all changed when the arm moves or what it predicts, and 100 ms of latency was reported to nearly halve success on moving targets (2611.03103). On 4 November a tactile reflex path acting within 10 ms cut the first force peak by 34.1% against a 20 ms delayed reflex (2611.04424). On 5 November the work turned to profiling the target board before optimizing (2611.06710), and on 6 November to cutting remote camera bit rate (2611.07411).

Human-facing studies measure the person's experience, which can move independently of the robot's physical outcome

The exosuit study on 2 November found perceived weight followed initial experience rather than muscle-load reduction (2610.41679), and the dog-navigation pilot found a legible manoeuvre was more stressful when executed with stops (2610.40921). On 3 November finger patterns changed perceived care without changing resistance to contact (2611.02517), and on 4 November a pragmatic alert was understood almost as well as a complete one (2611.03976). On 5 November operator workload and perceived safety diverged from speed in an ROV study (2611.06689), and a persona study found models and people disagree on robot presence in streets (2611.05954). On 6 November a humanoid policy made partner motion an explicit input (2611.08407).

Human data helps when its embodiment matches the robot, not simply when it is larger

Robot-free handheld demonstrations reached 78% average success over ten tasks on 4 November (2611.03815), and human-video-to-robot-video conversion lifted unseen-task success from 2% to 60% (2611.05163). On 5 November the egocentric scaling study reported a log-linear relation between hours and simulated robot success (2611.06817), and the tactile study found that a single physical cue, marker flow, carried most of the gain (2611.06673). On 2 November, action-free videos of new tasks more than doubled out-of-distribution success (2610.40748).

Runtime monitors and safety filters are gaining explicit bounds, and they must be checked under the shifts a robot will meet

On 2 November a risk gate reduced distracted-tracking collisions by 3.96 points (2610.41275). On 4 November a backup-horizon safety filter kept its guarantee under control bounds and cut median travel time 14% against recorded human drivers with no collisions (2611.04957), and a GPU reachability solver ran at 112 Hz inside the control loop (2611.04096). On 6 November a VLA failure-mode monitor reported bounds that held in up to 86% of unseen-task runs against at most 21% for baselines (2611.08425), and action-conditioned monitoring reached 83.0% accuracy against 74.5% for video-only (2611.07540).
