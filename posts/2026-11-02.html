The biggest manipulation gains today come from evolving the harness around a frozen model, while the papers that matter most for human-facing robots show policies that start moving when nobody asked, pick the wrong object in clutter, and change how people feel in ways that physical metrics miss.

Several groups now improve robots without touching policy weights: a coding model rewrites the control harness, a teacher's harness is distilled into a 4B student, or lessons from past runs are stored as context. That makes the evaluation harness part of the product, and today's evaluation papers show how often cheap proxies (offline loss, a clean build, a module's name, a VLM judge) disagree with what actually runs. The behaviours those proxies miss are the ones people notice first: every tested VLA checkpoint moved on an empty instruction, generalists acted on the wrong object 30% of the time in clutter, and dual-encoder VLMs scored at chance on single-attribute changes. On the human side, an exosuit study and a dog-navigation pilot both show that what a robot does physically does not predict how it is perceived, so perception and choices have to be measured directly. Human data keeps getting cheaper to use, since action-free video, contact geometry and object motion carry most of the transfer, and humanoid motion generators are being closed around robot feedback so they can respond to people mid-motion.

Insights

  1. Add a 'no task' suite before putting a VLA near people. In 2610.41609 every tested checkpoint started moving on an empty instruction, and a prompt prefix telling it not to move stopped none of them, while a gate comparing the first action chunk against the empty-instruction chunk held the robot in five of six checkpoints. Speech recognition errors, small talk and half-finished sentences all reach a human-facing robot as non-task text, so log what your stack does on them and implement the start-of-motion decision as an explicit node (for example a ROS 2 action server precondition) rather than relying on the policy.
  2. Measure what people perceive and decide, not only what the robot does to their body. The exosuit study (2610.41679) found perceived weight and effort-based choices were shaped by participants' initial experience, and the dog pilot (2610.40921) found a 'legible' manoeuvre became a stressor when executed with stops. In user studies, counterbalance condition order, record first exposure as a covariate, and replay the executed trajectory (not the planned one) when judging legibility.
  3. Try iterating on the harness before fine-tuning, but audit it. RoboArchitect (2610.42141) and TinyAstral (2610.41216) get large gains without changing policy weights, and TinyAstral only accepts an edit after a paired statistical test. The module audit (2610.41604) is the counterweight: 18 of 25 named modules never reached any output. If you add memory, lessons or 'theory of mind' components to a social robot, check with a simple ablation that each one changes behaviour, and version the harness together with the model.
  4. Do not pick dexterous policies offline. In 2610.40828, observation and action representation (cropping to a region of interest, robot-base frame, absolute rather than relative actions) moved success by tens of points while optimizer choices did not, and offline metrics only shortlisted. Fix representation choices first, then spend real-robot trials on the shortlist. The same lesson applies to LLM-assisted ROS 2 migration: in 2610.41168 most failures compiled cleanly, so require launch tests that exercise topics, services and actions.

VLA Policies Do Not Know When Not to Act: Unintended Task Execution and Start Suppression RO/AI
Minsu Jang, Jae-Woo Choi, Youngwoo Yoon, Dohyung Kim · arXiv:2610.41609
Why read it
A robot that moves when nobody asked is a direct safety and trust problem around people, and this is the first systematic test of it on current VLAs.
Key idea
Hold the initial state fixed, vary only the instruction (empty, neutral, nonsense, infeasible, wrong task), and ask whether the robot starts moving, which task it completes, and what the instruction actually determines.
Evidence
Six checkpoints in LIBERO-Goal simulation; with an empty instruction the robot started moving in every episode for all six, and five often completed an unrequested task; a 'do not move' prompt prefix stopped none, a first-chunk similarity gate held the robot on non-task inputs in five of six, and fine-tuning prevented task completion but did not reliably keep the robot still.
Caveat
Simulation only, in one benchmark suite; instructions are typed text rather than real speech, and no real robot or human study is reported.
Biomechanics do not predict perceptual and cognitive consequences of force augmentation RO
Maria Molina-Sanchez, Francesco Missiroli, Viktorija Pavalkyte, Mabel Ziman et al. · arXiv:2610.41679
Why read it
It shows directly, with human participants, that physical assistance metrics do not predict perception or decisions, which is the assumption behind most assistive-robot evaluations.
Key idea
Evaluate force augmentation on weight perception, adaptation to a changing environment and effort-based decision-making alongside muscle activity, and test whether the cognitive outcomes follow the biomechanical one.
Evidence
Elbow-assisting exosuit during lifting; assistance reduced muscle activity and perceived weight, but the weight effect depended strongly on initial experience, adaptation to changing contingencies was selectively reduced, and effort-based decisions followed initial experience rather than reductions in muscle activity or perceived fatigue.
Caveat
The abstract gives no participant count or demographics, and it covers one device and one lifting task; extending the finding to non-wearable robots is our inference.
From Teleoperation to Long-Horizon Dexterity: Evaluating Visuomotor Skills and Offline Proxies for Dexterous Manipulation RO/AI
Ammar N. Abbas, Aayush Jain, Prashanth Viswanath, Nivesh Gadipudi et al. · arXiv:2610.40828
Why read it
One of the largest real-robot ablations of imitation learning, and it tells you which design choices are worth your limited robot time.
Key idea
Run controlled ablations across many policy families on one hard real task, and test whether any offline metric can stand in for real-robot success.
Evidence
281 policies from seven families in about 1,700 real trials of RJ45 grasping and insertion with a five-fingered hand; ROI cropping, base-frame representation and in-encoder normalization each improved point-cloud policies by 70-100 points, and absolute actions improved VLA insertion by up to 70 points; only five of 67 offline metrics correlated with success (r = -0.70 to -0.80), and only within one family.
Caveat
A single insertion task on one hand; skill decomposition, multi-view perception and recovery demonstrations were associated with the best systems but not independently isolated.
RoboArchitect: Automating the Design of Robotic Agents with Persistent Memory RO/AI/CV
Shengguang Wu, Suning Huang, Cherie Ho, Ria Doshi et al. · arXiv:2610.42141
Why read it
It gives the clearest numbers today for how much a frozen policy can gain from an automatically revised harness, without any retraining.
Key idea
A coding model watches rollouts, diagnoses failures and rewrites the control code around a frozen policy (when to call it, when to use vision tools, when to command the arm directly), including a persistent memory it designs itself.
Evidence
Average success on held-out scenes rose from 9.7% to 43.3% and on held-out tasks from 12.7% to 40.2% across multiple benchmarks and policies; learned strategies include lifting a jammed arm clear and reaching in horizontally around obstacles.
Caveat
The abstract does not say whether evaluation is in simulation or on real hardware, or name the benchmarks; each new setup still needs rollouts to evolve its harness.
MolmoSpaces-CommonSense: Evaluating Commonsense in Robotic Manipulation Policies RO
Arjun Guru, Max Argus, Jae Sung Park, Jiafei Duan et al. · arXiv:2610.41377
Why read it
Its tasks are grounded in developmental cognitive science and target the clutter, occlusion and 'which part do I grab' reasoning that home and care robots face.
Key idea
Turn a developmental taxonomy of reasoning (occlusion, containment, support, affordances, hidden objects) into executable manipulation tasks, so policies must both recognize the structure and act correctly in it.
Evidence
Zero-shot generalist policies drop by over 50% in success as distractors increase, act on the wrong object 30% of the time in clutter, and select the correct object part only about 50% of the time even with specific prompts; VLM-based success estimation was also unreliable on these rollouts.
Caveat
Simulation only and zero-shot policies only; fine-tuned or harness-wrapped policies were not tested, so the gap for a deployed system is not measured.

  • Joint-Health-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Robust Robot Control Under Actuator Degradation RO/CV
    Haifa Zhang, Yijing Wang, Zhiqiang Zuo · arXiv:2610.41348
    Abstract

    Robots deployed in unstructured environments are susceptible to actuator-authority degradation, which reduces the effective control authority of individual actuators and can cause reinforcement learning policies trained under nominal dynamics to fail. Existing robustness approaches rely on discrete fault labels, broad domain randomization, or global latent variables, often without explicitly modeling continuous per-joint residual actuation capability. We propose a joint-health-aware reinforcement learning framework for robust robot control under simulated actuator degradation. Our framework encodes actuator condition in a continuous joint-health vector and models degradation as multiplicative attenuation of executed actions. A dedicated HealthNet estimator infers per-joint health from recent observations and actions, allowing the policy to redistribute control effort across healthy and degraded joints. We evaluate the method on Walker, Go2, Cassie, and Panda-based ManiSkill manipulation tasks under diverse degradation masks and severity levels. The method outperforms nominal and degradation-trained no-health baselines on the main evaluated metrics. Relative to the nominal baseline, task-level locomotion returns improve by up to 250.1\% and manipulation success rates by up to 15.2 percentage points; in the high-degradation Cassie group, return increases from 3.94 to 40.50 (+928.0\%). Ablations support the contribution of per-joint health estimation, action-degradation modeling, and health-conditioned control.

  • Physically Constrained Kinematic Calibration for High-Precision Robotic Needle Guidance RO
    Prabhu Nithin Gollapudi, Andreas Maier, Franziska Mathis-Ullrich, Water Zhao et al. · arXiv:2610.41429
    Abstract

    Percutaneous needle insertion is a common practice in minimally invasive procedures whose clinical success depends on accurate needle placement. However, achieving the necessary precision is a challenge for manual operators. Robotic assistance can improve targeting accuracy and reduce operator dependence, but residual millimeter-scale errors persist because nominal kinematic models do not fully represent the physical manipulator. This paper presents a robust bound-constrained kinematic calibration method designed to improve positional accuracy for a 6-Degree-of-Freedom (6-DoF) robotic needle-guidance manipulator. The approach uses physically plausible parameter ranges to regularize a compact 19-parameter geometric identification model. By treating these bounds as active optimization constraints rather than mere numerical safeguards, the framework effectively mitigates parameter coupling and overfitting, ensuring highly stable parameter identification. In simulation, the proposed method reduced end-effector Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) from 3.422 mm to 0.690 mm (a 79.8% improvement). In benchtop hardware experiments, the calibrated model achieved sub-millimeter validation accuracy, including 0.865 mm RMSE in experimental trajectory validation and 0.610 mm RMSE in a coordinate-lattice verification task. These results demonstrate that physically constrained kinematic calibration can enhance robotic needle-targeting accuracy, establishing a high-precision foundation for future clinical applications.

  • When the Past Shapes Action: Understanding Embodied Memory RO/AI
    Siqi Li, Ziyi Bai, Xinrun Xu, Tinglei Huang et al. · arXiv:2610.41688
    Abstract

  • Flexibility-Aware Robotic Manipulation: Deformation Estimation, Kinematics, and Control via Distributed Inertial Measurement RO
    Amir Hossein Barjini, Sadeq Yaqubi, Pauli Mustalahti, Jouni Mattila · arXiv:2610.41702
    Abstract

  • Suspended-Fabric Robotic Manipulation Surfaces: Modelling, Design Space, and Multimodular Extensio RO
    Pratik Ingle, Jørn Lambertsen, Kasper Støy, Andres Faiña · arXiv:2610.41982
    Abstract

    Robotic Manipulation Surfaces (RMS) reposition objects by deforming the surface on which they rest. A recent class of RMS suspends a continuous fabric sheet from a sparse grid of vertical linear actuators lowering the actuator density of such surfaces but how to design such surfaces so that objects can be controlled everywhere on it, and how to extend it beyond a single module so that objects can pass between modules and achieve parallel, remain open.   • Optimization Techniques for Solving the Minimum Red--Blue Separating Set Problem with an Application to Human--AI Text Separation AI

  • Hear No Evil: Detecting Gradient Leakage by Malicious Servers in Federated Learning AI
    Fei Wang, Baochun Li · arXiv:2506.20651
    Abstract

    Recent work has shown that gradient updates in federated learning (FL) can unintentionally reveal sensitive information about a client's local data.
