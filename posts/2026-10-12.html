Robot policies are getting explicit structure around the frozen model (revisable memory, failure probes judged by what they let you fix, and human data transferred as contacts), while new benchmarks show that spatial language from people is still where VLAs break.

Today's strongest manipulation results do not come from retraining the policy. Memory is being rebuilt as an auditable record with evidence links that can withdraw stale conclusions, and failure handling is judged by whether an alarm or probe leads to a useful intervention rather than by detection accuracy. World-model ideas are moving into training: predict the future to shape the policy's features, then skip or shrink that prediction at deployment. Human demonstrations transfer when they are expressed as contact goals, wrist-centred hand structure or latent actions rather than raw joint angles. On the human side, a real-robot benchmark finds VLAs execute the motions but lose spatial descriptions out of distribution, and interpretability work points to viewpoint transformation as the bottleneck, which is exactly what a robot needs when a person says "to my left". For a team building robots that talk with people of different ages and cultures, the practical move is to test spatial and perspective-dependent language explicitly, and to measure motion smoothness near people as its own outcome.

Insights

  1. Test spatial language separately from manipulation skill. Spa-Bench (2610.12696) shows the evaluated VLAs can do the motion but lose the referent when the spatial configuration is new, and S-Space (2610.13527) points to viewpoint transformation as the internal bottleneck. For a robot serving people across ages and cultures, who phrase locations differently and often speak from their own viewpoint, add an evaluation set of relational, ordinal, counting and speaker-relative instructions on your own robot, and log whether a rephrased direct instruction fixes the failure.
  2. Measure motion quality near people as its own outcome. JitterVLA (2610.13628) shows that irregular, delayed VLA command arrival produces jerky motion even when the predicted action is reasonable, and that simple interpolation cut modeled p99 jerk by 83% on human motion at a 2.0 cm tracking cost. Before running a user study with a VLA-driven arm, replay identical command streams through your controller options and compare jerk; perceived safety in HRI likely depends on this more than on task success.
  3. Stop scoring failure detectors on AUROC alone. RESCUE (2610.13516) finds that favourable detection scores did not establish lower intervention cost and that recoverability depends on the state and intervention pair. Pair each monitor with the recovery you actually run (pause and ask a person, retry, reset) and estimate the false-alarm rate your workflow can tolerate; for robots working among people, an unnecessary stop is also a social cost.
  4. When a person is in the loop, revisable memory is the piece to prototype first. REMM (2610.12710) reports that intervention recovery rose from 57% to 91% in mean task progress by withdrawing conclusions that an operator's disturbance invalidated, without retraining the executor. That pattern (evidence log, derived state, targeted recovery at skill boundaries) maps directly onto a behaviour-tree or ROS 2 action layer.

Spa-Bench: A Real-Robot Benchmark for Spatial Grounding in Vision-Language-Action Models RO
Justin Tien-Smith, Matthew Pidden, Rolandos Alexandros Potamias · arXiv:2610.12696
Why read it
It isolates spatial grounding from motor skill on a real arm, which is the failure a human-facing robot will hit first when people give relational or ordinal instructions.
Key idea
Keep objects, concepts and actions in the training distribution and vary only the spatial configuration, then add diagnostics for wording, object-task association and recognizing that no action is needed.
Evidence
4,092 episodes on an SO-101 arm (1,200 teleoperated demos, 2,892 evaluation rollouts from five VLAs); for three fully evaluated policies, success drops from 67.5-73.3% in distribution to 40.7-52.0% out of distribution, and matched direct instructions add 28.3-40.0 points.
Caveat
Six tabletop tasks on one low-cost arm with instructions from the benchmark authors; it does not test speaker-relative or culturally varied phrasing, so the gap for real users may be larger or different.
JitterVLA: A Replay Benchmark for Motion Quality in VLA-Driven Human-Robot Manipulation RO
Faith Ogunwoye, Didar Seyidov, Konstantin Gubernatorov, Dzmitry Tsetserukou · arXiv:2610.13628
Why read it
It gives a cheap, repeatable way to check whether a VLA-driven arm moves smoothly enough near people before you spend time on a user study.
Key idea
Replay identical command and arrival traces through different controllers so the controller's effect on jerk is isolated from the policy and the network delay.
Evidence
Synthetic reaches, hand trajectories from 20 people, three open VLA pipelines (838 episodes) and a UR3; interpolation lowered modeled p99 jerk by 83% (615 to 106 m/s^3) at 200 ms delay with a 2.0 cm tracking cost, replicated on 598 new episodes; on the UR3 accelerometer-derived jerk fell 3.0-14.0% in six exploratory pairs.
Caveat
Physical results are exploratory on one arm, and a position-derived metric did not resolve the contrast; no human perception or comfort ratings are reported, so the link to perceived safety is inferred.
RESCUE: Is This Alarm Worth Acting On? Counterfactual Evaluation of VLA Runtime Monitors RO/AI
David Ahmedt-Aristizabal, Abdelwahed Khamis, Mohammad Ali Armin, Lars Petersson · arXiv:2610.13516
Why read it
If you are adding a failure monitor to a deployed VLA, this changes how you should pick and tune it.
Key idea

REMM: Revisable Embodied Memory for Manipulation RO
Revanth Krishna Senthilkumaran, Ruwan Wickramarachchi, Alessandro Oltramari, Jonathan Francis · arXiv:2610.12710
Why read it
It shows a concrete supervisory layer that lets a frozen VLA recover after a person disturbs the task, which is the normal situation for robots working around people.
Key idea
Separate an append-only evidence history from revisable task state, link each derived condition to its supporting facts, and withdraw and repair only what a new observation invalidates.

Forward Dynamics Models RO
Zipei Ma, Junzhe Jiang, Jiahui Zhang, Juntong Zhang et al. · arXiv:2610.13117
Why read it
It is the clearest version today of getting world-model supervision without paying for future generation on the robot.
Key idea
Condition future-state prediction on action features (never the reverse), so ground-truth futures shape the policy during training, prediction errors cannot flow into actions, and the future branch is dropped at inference.

Instantiated with both VLM and video-generation backbones; 99.4% average success on LIBERO and absolute gains of up to 13% over VLA and WAM methods on real-world tasks.

The abstract does not describe the real-world tasks, robots or number of trials, and LIBERO is near saturation, so the size of the real-robot benefit is hard to judge from the abstract.

  • CASTER: Formalizing Zero-Shot Manipulation from Synthetic Videos as Constrained Optimization RO/CV
    Yinlong Dai, Homanga Bharadhwaj, Dylan P. Losey · arXiv:2610.12567
    Abstract

    Prior research on learning from humans often requires real human demonstrations. But advances in video generation models offer a new paradigm: we can now prompt these generators to make synthetic videos of the task. On the plus side, these synthetic videos take place in the robot's current environment, and do not require actual human teaching. However, while the generated videos appear to show plausible motions, they lack both physical consistency and robot grounding, and thus the robot cannot directly roll out the trajectory shown by the video. In this paper we seek to formalize the process of going from synthetic video generation to real-world robot trajectories for zero-shot manipulation tasks. We recognize that this problem is an instance of constrained optimization. The object trajectories extracted from the synthetic video provide an initial guess at the correct motion, and the robot must refine these trajectories (for instance, within a digital twin) while enforcing physical feasibility and semantic consistency. Our optimization-based formalism provides insights into the video generation properties necessary for success. Specifically, we show that the video generation must probabilistically seed the search within a semantic basin of attraction where the trajectory can be locally modified without losing its task alignment. Our analysis further reveals that state-of-the-art approaches can be viewed as instances of our unified formalism with key simplifications. In our experiments we remove these simplifications to more closely match the underlying optimization, leading to more robust zero-shot trajectories from synthetic videos and improving average task success by 34.1% compared to the best-performing baseline.   • Selectively Actuated Fiberbots for High-precision Minimally Invasive Surgery RO
    Jinshi Zhao, Libaihe Tian, Alex Ranne, Qindong Zheng et al. · arXiv:2610.12636
    Abstract

    Long, slender instruments are essential for minimally invasive surgery (MIS), but achieving precise distal manipulation becomes increasingly difficult when actuation extends along the entire instrument.     Jason Chen, I-Chun Arthur Liu, Gaurav Sukhatme, Daniel Seita · arXiv:2610.12734
    Abstract

    Stereo vision is increasingly used for robotic manipulation, with a growing body of work incorporating it into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.   • Tactile Sensing-based Secure and Gentle Grasping of Unknown Objects RO
    Feng Han · arXiv:2610.12790
    Abstract

    Reliable robotic manipulation requires maintaining a firm grasp while strictly avoiding both contact slip and structural damage to the object. Satisfying these inherent \textit{secure-and-gentle} grasping constraints is challenging for unknown objects. We present a novel online data-driven predictive control framework for safe dynamic robotic grasping of unknown objects. The proposed system features a pair of compact, single-channel tactile-sensing modules that characterize the gripper-object dynamic interaction in real time, entirely bypassing the need for prior knowledge or offline training. A finite-state-machine-based grasping force probing mechanism is developed to establish the initial grasping force and explicitly identify grasping force constraints. The proposed control framework runs at 100~Hz in real time to dynamically manipulate and handle objects rather than static holding. Extensive experimental evaluations across diverse contact surface topologies and various types of objects validate superior performance and efficiency compared to baseline strategies. The proposed method is able to manipulate objects ranging from delicate items, such as raw tofu and marshmallows, to rough objects, such as rocks.

  • ReAT: Residual Action Tokenization for Autoregressive Robot Policies RO
    Dijia Zhan, Zhuo Li, Xuemiao Xu, Sun Zheng et al. · arXiv:2610.13093
    Abstract

    Autoregressive robot policies rely on action tokenizers to convert continuous action chunks into compact sequences of discrete prediction targets. Existing tokenizers can reconstruct action chunks from discrete codes, but often leave implicit what each individual token contributes to the reconstructed action. We introduce Residual Action Tokenization (ReAT), which gives each token an explicit role in refining the current action reconstruction. It uses the decoded reconstruction as feedback to construct the next token from the remaining action error. This closes the loop between encoding and decoding, making each new token responsive to the errors left by the actual decoded prefix. Across 19 manipulation tasks spanning three simulation benchmarks and real-world settings, 8-token ReAT policies outperform the evaluated baselines in task success; on LIBERO, even 4-token ReAT surpasses the evaluated 8-token action-tokenization baselines at half the token budget. These results suggest a path toward action tokenization that supports self-corrective policy learning, helping autoregressive policies learn to progressively refine their own action plans.

  • Towards a Unified Taxonomy of Agricultural Harvesting Technologies: Linking Physical Interaction, Automation, and Selectivity RO
    Renato Cristiano Torres, Fabiano Mariath D Oliveira, Claudio Gottschalg Duque · arXiv:2610.13688
    Abstract

    Harvesting technologies have evolved from conventional mechanized systems based on bulk mechanical interaction toward increasingly automated and robotic systems capable of perception, decision-making, and localized actuation. This evolution has also created a classification problem, as harvesting systems are commonly described according to crop type, mechanical removal principle, sensing technology, robotic architecture, or degree of automation, making direct comparison difficult. This paper proposes a unified taxonomy for agricultural harvesting technologies based on two primary classification axes: the physical principle governing plant-machine interaction and the level of automation incorporated into the harvesting process. Selectivity is treated as an emergent functional attribute that expresses the biological and spatial scale at which perception and decision-making influence physical actuation. Conventional mechanized methods, including cutting, pulling, vibration, impact, brushing, pneumatic action, and combined systems, are analyzed together with semi-automated and robotic harvesting approaches. The analysis shows that increasing automation does not necessarily result in greater selectivity. A qualitative transition occurs when perception and decision-making directly influence product-level actuation, shifting harvesting from distributed or bulk interaction toward localized and selective manipulation. The proposed framework provides a common conceptual basis for positioning and comparing mechanized, automated, and robotic harvesting technologies. By separating the physical mechanism of product removal from the automation level and resulting selectivity, the taxonomy clarifies technological boundaries, identifies intermediate configurations, and supports the analysis and development of future harvesting systems.

  • What Visual Representations Do World Action Models Need? CV/RO
    Kai Liu, Jiangran Lyu, Tingyu Deng, Yixin Zheng et al. · arXiv:2610.13024
    Abstract

    World Action Models (WAMs) inherit powerful priors from pretrained video generation models, but their variational autoencoder (VAE) representations are optimized for visual reconstruction rather than manipulation. External visual representations can improve manipulation, but existing methods couple representation choices with different integration mechanisms, obscuring their individual effects. We systematically compare visual representations, five integration mechanisms, and feature combinations under controlled settings. Our study yields three main findings. First, strong general-purpose visual representations provide broader robustness gains and can match or outperform representations pretrained for specific visual capabilities. Second, channel-wise joint prediction achieves the highest average success rate; further analyses suggest that future-feature prediction and the action expert's effective use of added visual information contribute to its gains. Third, additional sources should be selected for the task-relevant information they contribute beyond existing representations, as the same source can help one combination but harm another. However, even the best evaluated composition provides a smaller additional gain than the initial improvement from adding a strong general-purpose representation to the VAE-only baseline. We instantiate the resulting Representation Recipe as R$^2$-WAM. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on RoboCasa GR1 Tabletop with an 84.7% success rate and reaches 92.8% on RoboTwin 2.0, improving over the VAE-only baselines by 5.9 and 2.2 percentage points, respectively. Taken together, these findings suggest that future work should prioritize stronger general-purpose visual representations and more effective mechanisms for integrating them into WAMs, with feature composition serving as a supplementary route to improvement.
