A one-day week (only Monday's listing was published) in which the strongest work built structure around frozen policies, while a real-robot benchmark showed that spatial language from people is still where VLAs break.

Monday 2026-10-12 was the only snapshot this week, so every trend below rests on a single listing and should be read as a signal rather than a confirmed pattern. It still continued the previous week's main thread: policies were improved from outside through revisable memory, failure monitors judged by the intervention they enable, and small adaptation modules on frozen weights. World-model ideas moved into training, so policies get supervision from predicted futures without paying for generation at deployment. Human demonstrations transferred as contacts, wrist-centred hand structure and latent actions rather than raw joint angles. For a team building robots that interact with people of different ages and cultures, the most relevant results were Spa-Bench, which shows VLAs losing spatial referents out of distribution, and JitterVLA, which gives a cheap way to check motion smoothness near people before a user study.

Trends

Manipulation memory is becoming an evidence log with revisable conclusions, not a longer frame buffer

On Monday four papers separated stored evidence from derived task state: REMM withdraws completed conditions when a disturbance invalidates them, GAMMA verifies every memory line against detector evidence, a dual-stream design splits progress from selective frame retrieval, and MeMoREval found frames and scene graphs beat generated captions. This follows the previous week's runtime-layer trend and is the piece most directly affected by people moving objects or undoing steps.

Papers: REMM, Gamma, Dual-Stream Memory for Vision-Language-Action…, What Should Embodied Agents Remember? Benchma…

Failure monitors are judged by the intervention they enable, and robots gather missing evidence themselves

Monday's RESCUE showed good detection scores do not imply lower intervention cost, with an ideal detector tolerating only 2.0-3.3% false alarms under stop-and-reset. Fly-by-Code wrote diagnostic probe programs when feedback was ambiguous, BT-Agent and EndoRSI turned structured failure feedback into the next revision, and Robot-WATCHDOG detected semantic failures without failure labels. Choose a monitor together with the recovery action you actually have.

Papers: RESCUE, Fly-by-Code, BT-Agent, EndoRSI, Robot-WATCHDOG

Future prediction moved to training time, so policies benefit from world models without running them on the robot

Five Monday papers used predicted futures to shape a policy and then dropped or shrank that prediction at inference: FDM conditions prediction on action features so it is skipped at deployment, IG-VLA cut per-chunk latency from 1081 ms to 169.5 ms with a gist token, Evo-World supervised with optical flow, SmolWAM ran at 57 ms per chunk, and LeWAM planned in a JEPA latent space. For on-robot compute this looks like the cheaper path to most of the benefit.

Papers: Forward Dynamics Models, Imagine the Future, Internalize the Gist, Evo-World, SmolWAM, LeWAM

Spatial language and motion quality, not motor skill, are where VLAs fall short around people

Monday's Spa-Bench found VLA success falling from 67.5-73.3% to 40.7-52.0% on unseen spatial configurations while motions stayed in distribution, S-Space located the weakness in viewpoint transformation, and SpatialASM trained perspective-taking explicitly. JitterVLA showed that irregular command arrival makes motion jerky even when actions are reasonable. Both fit the previous week's finding that instruction following, not skill, is where VLAs fail people.

Papers: Spa-Bench, S-Space, SpatialASM, JitterVLA

Human demonstrations transfer as contacts, wrist-centred hand structure and latent actions

On Monday PanTact recorded whole-body contact goals for a G1, VICAR kept contact poses as high-priority retargeting terms, Human2Dex raised two-hand success from 26.3% with raw human data to 85.8% through a shared wrist-centric representation, and LAPT-Scale learned latent actions from 23.3K hours of egocentric video. This repeats the previous week's contact-based transfer trend, so record contacts and timing explicitly when collecting human data.

Papers: PanTact, VICAR, Human2Dex, LAPT-Scale
