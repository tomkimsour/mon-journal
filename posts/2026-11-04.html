Insights

  1. Data is no longer the only gate for manipulation. AIRoA-0 shows a handheld gripper with wrist cameras can carry a bimanual system to 78% average success without robot data, and RoboTryOn shows synthetic robot video from human clips reaches 60% on unseen tasks. Try matching the capture gripper to the robot's fingers and camera set, and log which tasks were learned from human-only data. Stop assuming teleoperation is the only way to reach a new task.
  2. Safety and evaluation are moving into the runtime and the benchmark. Three papers attach guarantees or runtime filters to learned policies, and the evaluation papers show the nominal suite hides losses: LoopQ reports 1.5 to 4.3 points lost under perturbation, and RoboScope reports 359.7 minutes for a single 500-episode run. Try adding a perturbation suite and timing the evaluation before the next ablation sweep. Stop reading a clean nominal benchmark as evidence of robustness.
  3. For the HRI reader, 2611.03976 and 2611.05244 approach the same gap from opposite sides. A pragmatic alert is understood almost as well as a complete one, while a swarm that executes correctly can still read as threatening to observers. Try including an observer condition, not only the operator, in the next user study. Stop using task success as a proxy for how the interaction feels to the people around the robot.

  • A Tutorial on Shape Servoing of Deformable Objects-Part II: Advanced Concepts RO
    Ignacio Cuiral-Zueco, Alessio Caporali, Miguel Aranda, Gianluca Palli et al. · arXiv:2611.03825
    Abstract

    In robotics, shape control, or shape servoing, refers to the automatic manipulation of a deformable object toward a desired shape. This article is the second of a two-part tutorial on data-driven, Jacobian-based shape control, in which the deformation Jacobian, a local model of how robot motion changes the measured shape, is estimated from measurements. Part I built the shape-servoing loop and derived a local stability condition. This paper starts by using the singular value decomposition of the deformation Jacobian to identify shape changes that can be produced by small robot motions, those that require large robot motions, and those that are locally unavailable. It then analyses how the choice of shape features determines how the controller regulates the object's shape and, therefore, how much the object deforms on its way to the target. It then describes three ways in which the shape control loop can fail: loss of controllability, loss of observability, and occurrence of mechanical instabilities. It closes with shape-control extensions to time-varying targets, secondary tasks, and three-dimensional objects.

  • Self-Consistent Neural Control: Learning Trajectories for Implicit Equilibrium RO
    Hengyi Zhou, Haobo Fang, Xiaonan Huang, Dezhong Tong et al. · arXiv:2611.03885
    Abstract

    One big challenge in physical AI is interacting with systems whose states are defined by implicit equilibrium. Learning in these systems is difficult because their responses can be multistable, path-dependent, and sensitive over long horizons. Adjoint learning provides an efficient way to optimize trajectories without unrolling iterative equilibrium solvers, but its gradient relies on the frozen-tangent approximation, which neglects higher-order sensitivity terms and can become inaccurate when the local equilibrium varies rapidly. In this work, we introduce Self-Consistent Neural Control (SCNC), optimizing trajectories along which sensitivity variations are explicitly controlled. We derive a mathematical bound on the resulting gradient error and develop a practical finite-difference approximation. We validate the framework on deformable objects, which are representative of implicit equilibrium systems, ranging from simple quadratic energy models such as elastic rods to complex systems including thin-walled tubes, measuring tapes, and Slinky-like structures. Compared with the adjoint learning and the gradient-free iCEM baselines, SCNC achieves faster convergence and more stable optimization by reducing switching to undesirable equilibrium branches, doubling the stable optimization rate over adjoint learning 83.3% vs. 41.7% and reducing the average loss by more than 1.5 orders of magnitude on thin-walled tubes, where sensitivity variations are pronounced.

  • Grasping and Stabilizing Tumbling Objects with Rotating-Base Multi-Arm Space Manipulators RO
    Farhad Aghili · arXiv:2611.04176
    Abstract

    This paper presents an innovative guidance and control method for a space manipulator that actively maneuvers its base to hold a tumbling object stationary relative to the base during proximity operations. This simplifies motion planning, reduces collision risk, and enables impact-free capture using single- or multi-arm configurations, with or without predefined grasping interfaces. The proposed approach involves two phases. In the pre-grasping phase, a synchronization control aligns the target's center of mass (CoM) with the system's instantaneous center of rotation (ICR), virtually rigidizing the target-servicer system to keep the object stationary for reliable grasping. In the post grasping phase, a control strategy based on Hamiltonian dynamics and optimal control stabilizes the combined system by minimizing time, fuel, or energy, while respecting base torque limits and multiple grasping constraints. For structured targets, applied forces and torques stay within their bounds; for unstructured ones, friction constraints prevent slipping. A case study demonstrates the method's effectiveness in enabling both the subsequent grasping and detumbling phases.

  • Spectrally Routed Diffusion Policy for Multi-Task Robotic Manipulation RO
    Mingcong Li, Yihan Zhou, Jiayu Qu, Xueyang Guo et al. · arXiv:2611.04477
    Abstract

    Diffusion-based policies have achieved strong performance on single-task robotic manipulation. However, a single model trained jointly on multiple tasks consistently underperforms the per-task specialists trained on the same demonstrations. We focus on two challenges. First, the convolutional encoders commonly used in diffusion-based policies produce visual conditions that are not discriminative enough. Second, gradients from incompatible action modes update the shared denoiser's dense parameters along the same directions, causing destructive interference during joint training. To address both, we propose SR-DP (Spectrally Routed Diffusion Policy). A frozen VGGT encoder with a spatial-compression adapter produces compact multi-scale semantic-geometric tokens, a task-discriminative visual condition for tasks that share a workspace. On six RoboTwin tasks, SR-DP averages a 68.4% success rate, surpassing multi-task VO-DP by 21.8 points and the strongest per-task baseline by 3.8 points. On four real-world bimanual tasks, Stage 2 reaches a 43.0% average success rate and improves over Stage 1 by 19.0 points.

  • All-For-One: Distilling Visual Foundation Models to a Fast, Memory-Efficient Visuomotor Policy RO
    Byungkun Lee, Minho Park, Hojoon Lee, Dongyoon Hwang et al. · arXiv:2611.04485
    Abstract

    Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) provide semantic cues such as depth and segmentation useful for robotic manipulation, but directly deploying them in closed-loop control is impractical due to high computational cost, temporal inconsistency, and distribution shift between static internet images and egocentric robot observations.   • HiLo-WAM: Decoupling Long-Horizon Task Evolution and Local Dynamics in World Action Models RO
    Xingyu Wang, Yanjiang Guo, Zhijing Ding, Jiaxin Fang et al. · arXiv:2611.05023
    Abstract

    World Action Models (WAMs) improve robotic manipulation by jointly modeling visual dynamics and robot actions. However, most existing WAMs model dense short-horizon visual dynamics, capturing local interactions but providing limited long-range task context. Extending prediction horizons increases uncertainty and computation while entangling task-level evolution with fine-grained local dynamics, obscuring their dependencies and distinct roles in action generation. We propose HiLo-WAM, which decouples long-horizon task evolution from local visual dynamics while jointly modeling their interaction with robot actions. Ablations confirm the complementary contributions of long-horizon task evolution and local interaction dynamics, while temporal-scale analyses highlight the importance of balancing temporal coverage and resolution. Progressive inference further achieves a 2.93x speedup while improving performance.

    Abstract

  • PolicyIK: High-Precision Inverse Kinematics for Robot Arms with Reinforcement Learning RO
    Yuhan Liu, Xinqi Liu, Zixuan Dong, Keith W. Ross et al. · arXiv:2611.05372
    Abstract

Second, their robustness under distribution shift, a setting where structured inductive biases should confer advantages, remains unexplored. We propose a multi-resolution, higher-order Composite Generalised Additive Model (CoGA2M) and evaluate it on the TALENT benchmark suite. CoGA2M ranks in the top third across all baselines (29 for regression, 32 for classification), establishing competitive performance with state-of-the-art tabular methods. We further extend TALENT with out-of-distribution (OOD) splits exhibiting train-test distributional shift and benchmark CoGA2M against LightGBM, a consistently top-performing model on TALENT. Under distribution shift, CoGA2M outperforms LightGBM, demonstrating a clear structural advantage. Our results position spline-based GAMs as strong contenders for tabular prediction and reveal a previously unrecognised benefit of additive structure in the OOD regime.
