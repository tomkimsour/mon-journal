Today's most useful papers measure what actually limits a deployed robot (the demo-collection rig, motor heat, the decision a world model drives, the stage where a VLA fails) instead of the proxy metric the field usually reports.

Many of today's papers make the same move: they stop trusting the usual proxy and measure what actually limits deployment. On data, a study drawing on hundreds of thousands of hours shows that teleoperation latency, calibration and operator detours end up in the learned policy. Fast kinesthetic demos and a USD 557 hand-worn rig show that the collection interface sets the policy's ceiling. On world models, an independent audit and a decision-certificate method both show that one-step accuracy and visual plausibility do not predict whether a policy or plan built on the model works. VLA work is moving from one success rate to locating failures in a stage, a workspace region or a visual input. Legged deployments find that motor heat, not battery, ends sustained operation. For a team building robots that interact with people, the human-facing papers point the same way: graded risk around people, listening as well as looking in group conversation, and securing the configuration layer of LLM-driven social robots.

Insights

  1. Fix the data rig before buying more data. The Maniformer study's numbers (43-53% faster task completion with parallel body-part control, active-search demos taking arm selection from 50% to 100%, around 90% of detour length kept by the policy) and HandUMI's 0/20 at 101 demos versus 12/20 at 562 both say that interface and coverage decide results. Measure operator latency, calibration drift and effective workspace on your teleop setup this week, and keep a calibration-matched subset of every dataset.
  2. Stop accepting a world model on its prediction loss. The RWM audit (30% pruning keeps 90% of skill one step ahead but 39% at 2 s, ensemble uncertainty covering 4.6% of outcomes at one sigma) and CURE (regret 0.06 on trusted decisions versus 0.38 on others) both say the check must be the downstream decision. If you use a world model for policy evaluation, validate it by ranking known-good and known-bad policies, and for low-mounted cameras check that it keeps camera height.
  3. VLMs used as robot perception lose detail and depth before reasoning starts. CueClash finds frontier models answer 3-D relations from the picture plane (an 18 to 37 point shortcut gap), and the zoom study finds service-side downscaling shrinks small objects to a few pixels, while tiling raises accuracy by 11.1 to 14.5 points for Claude and Gemini. Pass ground-plane coordinates or crops to a VLM planner rather than a full-resolution frame, and treat an unsure reply as a trigger to zoom.
  4. LLM-driven social robots need ordinary network security before prompt-injection hardening. On a Reachy Mini Lite, eleven prompt-injection attempts were reported as resisted, but an unauthenticated management interface let an attacker replace the system prompt and personality, and 10 of 11 source-verifiable apps lacked authentication. Bind configuration endpoints to localhost and require authentication on any robot that talks to children or the public.

Hidden Variables Behind Real-Robot Data Quality: Lessons from Hundreds of Thousands of Hours RO
Maniformer Real-World Manipulation Team, Che Tsang Calvin, Xingyu Chen, Bowen Jiang et al. · arXiv:2611.22388
Why read it
It is the most concrete guide today to why real-robot data underperforms, from a team with a very large collection operation, and it maps directly to how a ROS 2 teleop stack is set up.
Key idea
Treat the collection system (embodiment workspace, teleop control scheme, calibration, observation completeness) as a variable that shapes the policy, and measure each one before scaling collection.
Evidence
Kinematic study of 25 dual-arm robots, controlled teleop experiments and fine-tuned pi0.5 evaluations: parallel body-part control cuts completion time 43-53%, compliant control improves success and reduces peak contact forces, active-search demos raise arm selection from 50% to 100%, inference-time miscalibration fails all 30 trials, and policies retain about 90% of detour path length.
Caveat
Results come from one team's dual-arm platforms and pi0.5 fine-tuning; mixed-calibration training is only shown usable 'in our tested configurations', and the abstract does not say how many tasks underlie each finding.
From Temperature-Limited to Battery-Limited Legged Locomotion RO
Raphaël Ségas, Balázs Opra, Léo Ghafari, Thomas Stewart · arXiv:2611.22231
Why read it
Any team running quadrupeds or humanoids for long shifts will hit this limit, and the fix is a reward term, not new hardware.
Key idea
Fit a lumped RC thermal model of the motors, then reward lowering the predicted steady-state temperature of the hottest motor, which responds within a gait cycle even though real temperatures change over minutes, so the policy needs no temperature input.
Evidence
On a Unitree Go2, the 13-node thermal network predicts all twelve motor temperatures with mean RMSE below 1 degree C across robots, gaits, payloads and speeds. With 5 kg at 1 m/s the baseline shuts down after 19 minutes (1.1 km) while the policy runs 174 minutes (10.0 km) until the battery is empty; on a factory circuit it completes 47 laps versus 25.
Caveat
Shown on one quadruped model in a 26 degree C room; the abstract does not report the cost in speed tracking or agility, or whether the thermal fit transfers to humanoids with different actuators.
Auditing a Learned Simulator for Legged Robots: The Robotic World Model on ANYmal D RO
Joyjeet Singh · arXiv:2611.23118
Why read it
If you train or evaluate locomotion policies in a learned simulator, this audit shows the checks you probably rely on can mislead you.
Key idea
Audit a learned simulator end to end on the decisions it supports (multi-step rollouts, uncertainty calibration and the policies trained inside it) rather than one-step prediction error.
Evidence
Rebuilt RWM for ANYmal D and measured 101 trained simulators: pruning 30% of weights keeps 90% of skill one step ahead but 39% at 2 s; ensemble disagreement ranks error well (Spearman +0.90) yet covers 4.6% of outcomes at one sigma; 13 of 18 better-predicting fine-tuned copies gave failing policies.
Caveat
The author states the scope: one robot, one gait, simulated data checked in a hand-built MuJoCo model, no hardware; the registered tests of which check ranks simulators by policy quality were not settled.
CoBoVLA: Decoupling Language from Action with Discrete Codes for Robust and Interpretable Robot Manipulation CV/RO
Mehul Arora, Dhruv Agarwal, Ambuj Agrawal · arXiv:2611.22395
Why read it
It shows that a small VLA with explicit stages can be both more robust to how people phrase requests and easier to debug, which matters for robots taking instructions from varied users.
Key idea
Split the VLA into a text encoder producing discrete step codes, a visual planner producing action codes, and a language-independent actor, so paraphrase robustness improves and each failure can be traced to a stage.
Evidence
250M parameters, 17 ms per control step; 91.4% on LIBERO-Goal versus 82.8% for SmolVLA under a matched protocol; 81.1% on 4,092 unseen LIBERO-Para paraphrases versus 36.5% for paraphrase-tuned SmolVLA; 91% success when instructions are encoded correctly versus 27.2% otherwise.
Caveat
All results are on LIBERO simulation suites; no real-robot test is reported, and paraphrase robustness is measured on LIBERO-Para wording, not on spoken or multilingual requests.
Nunchi: Ears Help Eyes in Multi-Party Conversations CV
JunYeong Park, Shijing Wang, Seonwook Park, Michael Stengel et al. · arXiv:2611.23115
Why read it
Knowing who a person is attending to in a group conversation is a core HRI skill, and this paper shows speech cues are where the remaining gain is.
Key idea
Turn gaze-model output, speaker detection and the transcript into one prompt with shared person labels, and let a VLM weigh gaze geometry against conversational cues to pick the attended person.
Evidence
New SORBET benchmark with 3,340 samples from 1,578 clips with synchronized speech, transcripts and attended-person labels; Nunchi beats the strongest existing method by 4.8 percentage points, gains hold across three VLMs, with automatic transcripts and on two existing datasets, and extra visual cues do not help.
Caveat
Evaluated on recorded clips rather than a robot in the conversation; the abstract does not report latency, and it is unclear how well the approach handles languages or cultures not in the clips.

  • Progress Predicts Policy RO
    Licheng Zhong, Gim Hee Lee · arXiv:2611.22491
    Abstract

    A task specifies how the world should change, while a policy specifies what the robot should do. We introduce Progress Predicts Policy (PPP), which learns a language-conditioned scalar field through the commands induced by its differential. We obtain action conditioning by composing a latent progress field with a shared world model and read its action gradient at a fixed reference command. Continuous commands scale this gradient by a learned positive gain, while discrete commands use finite differences of the same scalar. This yields a hybrid policy without a separate action head or iterative search. Fitting demonstration progress values alone does not ensure that action gradients align with expert commands. PPP trains the induced commands by imitation and uses double backpropagation for the continuous gradient readout to shape the field's local control geometry. Demonstration timing calibrates field values on observed states without additional annotation. The continuous readout remains learnable without progress labels but these timing-derived targets support progress monitoring during execution. Experiments in simulated and real robot manipulation show that PPP outperforms behavioral cloning under a shared architecture.

  • Learning Accurate End-Effector Trajectory Tracking with Inertial Feedback for a Quadruped Manipulator RO
    Dohyeon Kim, Seungyong Han, Yeongtae Jung · arXiv:2611.22695
    Abstract

    Accurate end-effector trajectory tracking on quadruped manipulators is challenging because uncertainties in actuators, foot-ground contact, and geometric errors are propagated through the leg-arm kinematic chain. Detecting such errors requires sensing beyond joint encoders that can capture the effects of these uncertainties on the robot's motion, since joint encoders only provide measurements at the actuated joints and cannot fully observe mechanical errors such as backlash, dimensional inaccuracies, or structural deformation. This work addresses these challenges using a reinforcement learning framework with end-effector (EE) inertial feedback, targeting local high-accuracy manipulation while maintaining a nominal body pose and support configuration without intentional body motion or foot repositioning. The learned policy uses EE inertial measurements to compensate for tracking errors induced by uncertainties throughout the leg-arm system that cannot be fully inferred from proprioception alone. Simulation and real-world experiments on continuous EE trajectory tracking show overall improvements relative to nominal and proprioception-only baselines. In real-world experiments, the proposed policy reduces the MoCap-based RMSE by 17.3% and 28.0% relative to the nominal baseline under the two in-distribution (ID) command conditions, respectively, and by an additional 12.6% and 15.9% relative to the proprioception-only baseline. Furthermore, the trajectory-wise RMSE between FK-reconstructed and motion-capture-measured end-effector positions averaged 12.61+-2.15mm (mean+-standard deviation) across 24 controller-trajectory combinations, revealing a substantial discrepancy between nominal kinematic reconstruction and measured end-effector motion.

    Abstract

  • Physics-Informed Tactile-based Shape Estimation of Deformable Linear Objects RO
    Rotem Atari, Stav Ashur, Avishai Sintov · arXiv:2611.22990
    Abstract

    Accurate shape estimation of Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs) is a fundamental challenge in robotic manipulation, often hindered by visual occlusions and the high-dimensional configuration space of flexible materials. While Force/Torque (F/T) and vision-centric methods have been proposed, they frequently struggle with grasp-site ambiguities and environmental interference. In this letter, we investigate the hypothesis that global shape estimation can be achieved through tactile sensing and proprioception, without any visual perception. We contend that dense, high-resolution tactile imprints capture critical boundary conditions and contact gradients that are sufficient to resolve the high-dimensional configuration of the DLO. Experimental results demonstrate that our tactile-based approach achieves superior reconstruction accuracy across Euclidean and Chamfer metrics. Furthermore, we demonstrate the efficiency of this modality, achieving model transfer to novel DLO materials and geometries. Our findings suggest that tactile feedback provides a robust and computationally efficient alternative for DLO state estimation in visually degraded or occluded environments.
